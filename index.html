Introduction

What is this?

Ethics Under Attack is a 4-player educational card game based on Global Issues in Engineering Ethics. Each player protects four Ethics Cards representing core professional values — while attacking to break down opponents' values with real-world ethical failures.

The Goal

Destroy your opponents' Ethics Cards using matched Attack cards, defend your own with Defense cards, and repair the damage when you can. Outlast the table to win.

Game Setup

Each player places four Ethics Cards face-up: Business, Computer, Environmental, and Engineering Management.
Shuffle the 64-card Main Deck and deal 5 cards to each player.
Shuffle the 10-card Ethics Policy Deck and reveal the top card. It becomes the active policy.
The remaining Main Deck becomes the Draw Deck. You're ready to play.

🟪 Ethics Policy Cards
Do Not Use a Computer to Harm Other People
Duration: Until replaced

A player cannot target the same opponent with an Attack Card on two consecutive turns. If they try, the Attack Card is discarded without effect and their turn ends immediately.

Do Not Interfere with Other People's Work
Duration: Until replaced

A player may not attack the same opponent on consecutive turns — they must target someone else first. Violating attacks fail and are discarded.

Do Not Snoop Around in Other People's Files
Duration: Until replaced

Whistleblower may only look at an opponent's hand — it cannot steal a card. Government Audit lets the player look at the opponent's hand and shuffle one chosen card back into the Main Deck, instead of discarding two random cards.

Do Not Use a Computer to Steal
Duration: Until replaced

Whistleblower and Intellectual Property Theft cannot be played — either card is immediately discarded without effect if attempted.

Do Not Bear False Witness
Duration: Until replaced

Fraudulent Report no longer destroys Business Ethics. Instead, the targeted player skips their Draw Phase on their next turn.

Do Not Copy Software You Did Not Pay For
Duration: Until replaced

Data Breach and Hacking become temporary — instead of destroying Computer Ethics, the card is turned sideways and automatically returns face-up at the start of the owner's next turn.

Do Not Use Other People's Intellectual Output
Duration: Until replaced

If Intellectual Property Theft is blocked by a Defense Card, the attacking player must discard 2 additional cards from their hand.

Think About the Social Consequences of the Program You Are Writing
Duration: Until replaced

Whenever a player successfully destroys an opponent's Ethics Card, they must immediately discard 1 random card from their own hand.

Always Consider the Public Good
Duration: Until replaced

At the start of their turn, each player chooses one: draw 1 card, or repair 1 destroyed Ethics Card for free — not both.

Use Computers with Consideration and Respect
Duration: Until replaced

Before an Attack Card resolves, the targeted player may discard 1 card from their hand. If they do, the attacker must choose a different opponent or end their turn without attacking.

Optional Rule: The Joker Pack

For players looking for a more chaotic and unpredictable experience, Ethics Under Attack includes an optional Joker Pack inspired by the classic Joker cards found in standard playing card decks. These cards have no gameplay effect — they cannot be played, discarded for an effect, used as a defense, or counted as any card type. Their only purpose is to occupy space in a player's hand, forcing them to manage with fewer useful cards.

Optional Rule: Before shuffling the Main Deck, players may unanimously agree to include the Joker Cards. If included, shuffle them into the Main Deck. Otherwise, leave them out of the game.

These Joker Cards feature the members of the development team and serve as a fun Easter egg for players. They are completely optional and exist purely to add humor and a small element of hand-management challenge without affecting the educational mechanics of the game.

Effect

  • 🃏 A Joker Card has no effect when played.
  • 📥 It cannot be used to attack, defend, repair, or activate any special ability.
  • 🗑️ It simply occupies a space in your hand until you choose to play and discard it during your Play Phase.

Because they provide no strategic benefit, Joker Cards act as "dead" cards that reduce the number of useful cards in your hand, adding a lighthearted challenge for players who choose to include them.

Frequently Asked Questions

🎮 General Gameplay
Q1Do I have to draw a card at the beginning of every turn?

Yes. Drawing 1 card during the Draw Phase is mandatory unless an Active Ethics Policy or another card specifically changes this rule. Every turn begins with the Draw Phase before any other actions can be taken.

Q2Can I skip playing a card after drawing?

Yes. After drawing your mandatory card, you may choose to:

  • 🟥 Play one Attack Card
  • 🟦 Play one Repair Card
  • 🟨 Play one Special/Event Card
  • Play no card at all

Choosing not to play a card allows you to save it for a later turn.

Defense Cards cannot be played during your own turn unless another card specifically states otherwise.

Q3When do players receive new cards?

Players receive new cards in the following ways:

  • Draw 1 mandatory card at the beginning of every turn.
  • Play Special/Event Cards that instruct them to draw additional cards (such as Budget Increase or Multinational Expansion).
Q4What happens if the Main Deck runs out of cards?

If the Main Deck becomes empty:

  • Shuffle the entire Discard Pile.
  • Create a new Main Deck.
  • Continue drawing cards normally.

Ethics Cards and Ethics Policy Cards are never shuffled into the Main Deck.

🟪 Ethics Policy Questions
Q5Under "Do Not Use a Computer to Harm Other People," what happens if I attack the same opponent on consecutive turns?

While this policy is active, you cannot attack the same opponent on two consecutive turns. If you attempt to do so:

  • Your Attack Card is immediately discarded.
  • The attack has no effect.
  • The targeted player does not play a Defense Card.
  • Your turn immediately proceeds to the End Phase.
ExampleTurn 1: Attack Player B ✅
Turn 2: Attack Player B again ❌
The second attack is cancelled because you attacked the same opponent on your previous turn.
Q6What is the difference between "Do Not Use a Computer to Harm Other People" and "Do Not Interfere with Other People's Work"?

Although both policies prevent repeatedly targeting the same opponent, they work differently.

Do Not Use a Computer to Harm Other People

This policy only checks your previous turn. You cannot attack the same opponent on two consecutive turns.

Your TurnActionResult
Turn 1Attack Player B✅ Allowed
Turn 2Attack Player B❌ Not Allowed
Turn 3Attack Player B✅ Allowed

Since you did not attack Player B on your previous turn, you may attack them again.

Do Not Interfere with Other People's Work

This policy requires you to attack a different opponent before attacking the same player again. Skipping your Attack Phase, playing a Repair Card, playing a Special/Event Card, or simply ending your turn does not reset this restriction.

Example — AllowedTurn 1: Attack Player B ✅
Turn 2: Attack Player C ✅
Turn 3: Attack Player B ✅
Because you attacked another opponent before returning to Player B.
Example — Not AllowedTurn 1: Attack Player B ✅
Turn 2: Play Budget Increase ✅
Turn 3: Attack Player B ❌
You never attacked another opponent.
Example — Not AllowedTurn 1: Attack Player B ✅
Turn 2: Repair an Ethics Card ✅
Turn 3: Attack Player B ❌
Repairing or passing your turn does not satisfy the policy.
Q7Under "Do Not Snoop Around in Other People's Files," how are Whistleblower and Government Audit affected?

This policy changes the effects of both cards.

Whistleblower

Normally, you steal one card from an opponent. While this policy is active: look at the opponent's hand — you cannot steal any cards.

Government Audit

Normally, the opponent discards 2 random cards. While this policy is active: look at the opponent's hand, choose one card, and shuffle that card back into the Main Deck.

The chosen card is not discarded, and the opponent does not draw a replacement card.

Q8Under "Do Not Bear False Witness," who skips the Draw Phase?

The targeted player skips the Draw Phase during their next turn. Instead of destroying Business Ethics, Fraudulent Report temporarily prevents that player from drawing their mandatory card.

They may still:

  • Play cards already in their hand.
  • Use Defense Cards.
  • Continue the rest of their turn normally.
Q9Under "Do Not Copy Software You Did Not Pay For," what happens if Computer Ethics is disabled and my other Ethics Cards are destroyed?

A disabled Computer Ethics card is not considered destroyed. If Data Breach or Hacking disables your Computer Ethics:

  • Turn the card sideways.
  • It remains one of your Ethics Cards.
  • At the beginning of your next turn, it automatically returns face-up.

You are not eliminated while your Computer Ethics is disabled.

Q10Under "Use Computers with Consideration and Respect," what happens if the targeted player discards a card?

Before your Attack Card resolves, the targeted player may discard one card. If they do:

  • You must immediately choose another valid opponent using the same Attack Card.
  • If another valid opponent exists, the attack continues against the new target.
  • If no valid opponent exists, the Attack Card is discarded without effect.

You do not play another Attack Card or return the Attack Card to your hand.

🟨 Special/Event Card Questions
Q11When I play Budget Increase, do I draw my normal card first?

Yes. Every turn always begins with the mandatory Draw Phase.

ExampleDraw 1 card during your Draw Phase → Play Budget Increase → Immediately draw 2 additional cards.

The two cards gained from Budget Increase are extra cards and do not replace your normal draw.

Q12Can Multinational Expansion ignore the current Active Ethics Policy?

No. If you choose the option to play one additional Attack Card, that Attack Card must still follow:

  • The normal game rules.
  • The current Active Ethics Policy.

For example, if an Ethics Policy prevents you from attacking a certain opponent, Multinational Expansion does not override that restriction.

🏆 Winning the Game
Q13When is a player eliminated?

A player is eliminated only when all four Ethics Cards are destroyed. A disabled Computer Ethics card (from the Do Not Copy Software You Did Not Pay For policy) is not considered destroyed and still counts as an active Ethics Card.

Q14When does the Active Ethics Policy change?

The Active Ethics Policy changes when either of the following occurs:

  • Three complete rounds have been played, or
  • A player uses the Revised Code of Ethics Special/Event Card.

When the policy changes:

  • Discard the current Active Ethics Policy.
  • Reveal the next Ethics Policy from the Ethics Policy Deck.
  • Its effects immediately apply to all players.

If the Ethics Policy Deck becomes empty, shuffle the discarded Ethics Policy Cards to create a new Ethics Policy Deck and continue playing.

This FAQ covers the most common gameplay questions and clarifies how card effects, Ethics Policies, and turn mechanics interact during the game.
